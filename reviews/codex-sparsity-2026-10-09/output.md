1. **Kilgarriff: mostly right, but avoiding zero-null tests doesn’t answer his deeper objection.** Threshold rules still classify evidence; substantively justified thresholds improve that classification without establishing motivation. Measured controls and simulations assess specified alternatives, not arbitrary association generally. Convergence helps only insofar as tests don’t share lexical selection, commonness instruments or descriptive biases. L1, L2 and production aren’t automatically independent corroboration. The plan appropriately limits causal claims, but “Which rarity” supplies another reason for restraint: actual prevalence remains dependent on identifying assumptions. ([analysis_plan.md](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/development/marked-pole-stereotypes/analysis_plan.md), “H2 details,” “Which rarity,” “Multiverse.”)

2. **Dunning: the likelihood choice addresses his specific approximation complaint; pooling doesn’t guarantee reliable sparse-data inference.** Prior sensitivity and calibration under misspecification remain essential. More importantly, the proposed frequency fix conflates two uncertainties. In `build_joint`, `sig_f` represents unexplained *between-word variation* in log frequency given prevalence, stakes, valence and marking; it isn’t explicitly corpus sampling error. Replacing it with count-based SEs would erase that substantive residual variation.

   Prefer two layers: latent true log usage rate with its own residual variation, then corpus counts conditional on that rate and exposure. Feed the latent rate into production too: currently observed `f` enters production directly. Negative-binomial sampling may accommodate overdispersion, but aggregate counts alone cannot establish document burstiness. The \(1/\sqrt{\text{count}}\) approximation is unreliable at very small counts and undefined at zero. Keep corpus representativeness and sense mismatch separate from sampling error. Also, D2 calibrates lexical models; the revised production likelihood requires D1c. ([d1.py](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/development/marked-pole-stereotypes/scripts/d1/d1.py:174), `build_joint`; plan, “H2 details,” “D2.”)

3. **No-extant-root words: separate coding is sensible; automatic L1 inclusion isn’t.** Excluding them defines a restricted population, rather than necessarily biasing inference *within* extant-root pairs. Selection threatens generalization beyond that population.

   Constructing a “modern opposite” changes the opposition being tested and introduces analyst discretion. Use a separately reported exploratory stratum only with a frozen antonym-selection rule, independent semantic adjudication, ambiguity reporting and alternative-opposite sensitivity. Opposite selectors should be blind to commonness/frequency results and study predictions; subsequent commonness judges should see randomized descriptions without lexical forms. Selectors cannot be fully blind to morphology while interpreting the source word. Positive negated words with extant eligible roots already supply discriminating cases; positivity doesn’t justify manufactured pairs. (Plan, “L1: Frame,” “Descriptions and judges”; “L2: Model.”)

Finally, root loss doesn’t itself establish that frequency asymmetry reversed or caused survival. Those are historical hypotheses requiring evidence. D2’s root-availability scenarios test consequences of assumptions, not their historical truth.
