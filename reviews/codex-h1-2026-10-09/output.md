**Keep H1 only as an exploratory descriptive contrast, not as a discriminating test of the common-cause account.** The recommendations improve measurement, but overstate what adjustment can establish. Also, this remains a skeleton rather than a fixed preregistration ([analysis_plan.md:4](analysis_plan.md:4)).

1. **5a — Agree with the association; disagree with the causal justification.** Register and act type are not demonstrably antecedent merely because they describe words. Act type may partly reflect the meaning created by negation; contemporary register may reflect lexical history and use. Specify competing causal structures before choosing adjustment. “Pure common cause predicts zero” requires adequately measured rarity, no additional shared causes, and the appropriate frequency treatment. A positive residual does not establish an effect of marking.

   Define the predictive comparison precisely: average marked–unmarked differences over a common-support distribution of words and groups, keeping the competing vocabulary fixed. Specify whether frequency is held fixed or integrated over its marking-dependent distribution; those estimate different quantities. D1 explicitly includes marking → frequency → production ([README:26–31](scripts/d1/README.md:26)).

2. **5b — Mostly agree, with restraint.** Report formal negation and act type separately. Pool affix effects only with a defensible exchangeability assumption: contrary prefixes, *non-* and privative *-less* differ semantically and morphologically ([analysis_plan.md:80](analysis_plan.md:80)). Use a skeptical interaction prior and check recovery under sparse overlap.

   Coder-family vote share is **not** a calibrated probability of act type, and entering it continuously assumes a graded predictor rather than propagating classification uncertainty. Prefer a calibrated measurement model allowing correlated family errors and genuinely ambiguous cases. More hierarchy cannot manufacture information.

3. **5c — Agree with noisy-control simulation; disagree with equating etymology and register.** Latinate origin is a proxy for the proposed mechanism, not its measurement. Distinguish origin, contemporary register and familiarity; document what each coding actually captures. A dictionary source can be accurate about ancestry while missing the confound completely.

   Scenario 2 assumes a particular factor independent of valence with fixed loadings ([README:33](scripts/d1/README.md:33)). Extend it across control reliability, correlations with valence/act type, and differential coding errors. This quantifies vulnerability under assumptions, not whether actual confounding has been removed.

4. **5d — Agree with independent human calibration; disagree with “about 10%” as a design.** Choose sample size through anticipated precision for consequential error rates. Stratify by affix, valence, disagreement and rare act-type categories; retain sampling weights or model selection. Include multiple blinded human coders and adjudication, without treating humans as infallible.

   If humans see the words, they cannot be blinded to morphology; they can be blinded to hypotheses and model codes. Explicit instructions should prevent “prefixed” from becoming shorthand for “omission.” The French evidence concerns a different language and outcome and lacks human validation ([analysis_plan.md:50](analysis_plan.md:50)).

5. **5e — Agree with avoiding a survival rule; revise the robustness table.** These four specifications are not strictly nested: register-only and act-type-only are alternative branches. Report standardized predictive contrasts on the same target population, not just coefficients, alongside overlap and prior sensitivity. Coefficient movement is not automatically confounding removal.

   The pilot demonstrates possible bias: large-sample marking estimates are .203 [.028, .379] and .198 [.018, .380] ([pilot2_fits.csv:107](results/d1/pilot2_fits.csv:107), [123](results/d1/pilot2_fits.csv:123)). It supplies neither empirical false-positive probabilities nor a correction factor. At true zero, exaggeration ratios are undefined. Twenty repetitions yield coarse calibration, and the categorical replacement requires new simulation ([analysis_plan.md:22](analysis_plan.md:22)).

**What I would retain instead:** an exploratory question: *Does formal negation improve prediction of word choice beyond measured lexical properties, within supported comparisons?* Evaluate prediction by holding out words or morphological families; participant-only holdouts leave the word-level identification problem largely untouched. Keep L1/L2 as the direct investigations of rarity–marking linkage. Predictive improvement remains compatible with confounding.

Two missed problems matter. First, negative unprefixed adjectives are informative contrasts too: predominantly negative marked words do not themselves establish near-determinism by valence. Inspect overlap before accepting the plan’s claim that identification rests on a small off-diagonal set ([analysis_plan.md:69](analysis_plan.md:69), [88](analysis_plan.md:88)).

Second, the frequency paragraph is incorrect: jointly modelling frequency does not stop the production equation from conditioning on it ([analysis_plan.md:35](analysis_plan.md:35), [54](analysis_plan.md:54)). As the previous review recognized for H2, interpretation depends on the predictive averaging operation ([previous review:5](reviews/codex-h2-2026-10-09/output.md:5)). Generative modelling helps expose these assumptions; it does not resolve them.
