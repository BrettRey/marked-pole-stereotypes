Context. H1 asks whether formally negated trait words (un-, in-/im-/il-/ir-, dis-, non-, privative -less) are produced as stereotypes more often than within-valence rarity predicts. Under the hypothesis's pure common-cause account (rarity drives both marking and stereotyping), the marking coefficient should be about zero. D1 scenario 2 adds an unmeasured "register" factor (learned/Latinate words: less frequent, more often prefixed, more often produced); with H1's true value 0, the pilot fits gave the marking coefficient about +.20 with 90% intervals excluding 0 at the large data size, under both prevalence indicators, while the rarity coefficient stayed near 0. A separate study (French, Google Books 1950-2019) found that whether a bad trait word defers to its antonym in comparatives follows act type (failing to do an expected act vs doing acts; logit 1.06 [0.51, 1.56], 128 concepts, act type coded by eight language-model families with no human check), and that formal negation had no detectable effect among traits once act type was in the model (-0.13 [-0.67, 0.54], 21 prefixed traits).
