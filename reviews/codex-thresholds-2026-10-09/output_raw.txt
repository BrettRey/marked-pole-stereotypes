- **Keep posterior comparisons and exceedance curves primary.** The 90% interval rule adds another cutoff alongside T. Specify interval construction; for equal-tailed intervals, “wholly beyond T” amounts to posterior probability above .95. Pre-registration constrains selective reporting; it doesn’t establish substantive validity. ([analysis_plan.md, “H2 details,” “What counts against H2”](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/development/marked-pole-stereotypes/analysis_plan.md:20))

- **A magnitude threshold doesn’t repair causal interpretation.** “Nonzero associations are unsurprising, therefore use substantive thresholds” is insufficient: a large association can reflect confounding or instrument bias. Separate magnitude, robustness to assumptions, and bearing on the mechanism. ([“What associations can and can’t show”](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/development/marked-pole-stereotypes/analysis_plan.md:41))

- **Don’t force a smallest-important-effect T.** Without a consequential decision or a theory predicting minimum magnitude, there may be no defensible unique value. Brett can specify an explicitly evaluative reporting benchmark, but it shouldn’t become the hypothesis’s implied commitment. First freeze each contrast, population, and averaging distribution. H2’s log-scale .10 needs a precise mapping to categorical-choice probabilities; L1’s θ_m already measures a probability difference; the word-level comparison needs fixed commonness endpoints. These require separate benchmarks. Show concrete probability examples and elicit which differences Brett regards as noteworthy, independently of detectable size. If none has a principled interpretation, retain the curves and omit three-way verdicts. ([“L1,” “What counts against”](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/development/marked-pole-stereotypes/analysis_plan.md:119); [“L2,” “What counts against the marking claim”](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/development/marked-pole-stereotypes/analysis_plan.md:126))

External hints support weak-effect scenarios, not numerical priors; UWA magnitudes don’t automatically transfer to lexical or judge contrasts. Model-protocol importance also differs from importance for human commonness.

D1/D2 should show label probabilities **by generating effect and bias scenario**; aggregating requires explicit weights. Exaggeration is undefined at zero and unstable nearby, so include conditional absolute bias.

Finally, “specification-dependent” conflates unanimous uncertainty with conflicting estimates. Separate them, and estimate dataset differences jointly where defensible; differences between identifying assumptions remain sensitivity contrasts.
