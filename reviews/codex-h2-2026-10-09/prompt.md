Context in brief: the project tests whether stereotype content concentrates on the "marked pole" of evaluative oppositions, because rarity drives both diagnosticity (Unkelbach, Weitzel & Alves 2026) and overt morphological coding. H2: at the same valence, rarer traits are more often produced as stereotypes. The joint model treats within-valence rarity as a latent variable measured by indicators (a prevalence norm with known item-level SEs; word frequency, which also has a direct accessibility effect on production). Production counts per group x trait word are negative binomial with group intercepts and item effects. Datasets: UWA Studies 1-2 (free-response stereotypes "you know exist in society", UK/US; Study 2 has 1,783 responses, 40 groups, one response per participant per group), Nicolas, Bai & Fiske 2022 (about 14,400 attributes, 43 groups, six per participant per group, US), Ingendahl et al. 2025 (seven countries, translated into local languages; only the Irish subsample is English).
