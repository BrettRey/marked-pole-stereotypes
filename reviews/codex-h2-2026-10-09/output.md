I broadly support 4b–4d. I’d revise 4a’s interpretation and replace 4e’s decision rule. **This is not yet a fixed preregistration:** the plan calls itself a skeleton, and its exclusions, models and falsification sections remain unfinished (`analysis_plan.md`, opening; “Data and exclusions”; “Models”; “What would count against each hypothesis”).

**4a — Agree on a marginal association as primary; disagree with calling it an identified total causal effect.** H2’s wording supports asking whether rarer traits receive more production at the same valence. Frequency’s causal position remains uncertain; modelling it jointly doesn’t establish mediation. Nor does holding frequency fixed isolate diagnosticity.

There’s also a technical mismatch: D1’s \(c_z=\beta_z+\beta_f\lambda_z\) holds marking fixed, although rarity predicts marking and marking predicts frequency and production. It therefore omits paths through marking (`scripts/d1/README.md`, “Generating model”; “v2.1 and v2.2”). Define the primary contrast through posterior prediction, averaging over frequency **and marking** under an explicit joint model. Label it a model-based marginal association unless causal assumptions are defended. Report conditional coefficients secondarily.

**4b — Mostly agree.** Study 1 is a sensible calibration set, provided its outcomes don’t guide denominator construction or model selection. English-only Ingendahl is a defensible scope restriction. Treat UWA Study 2 and Nicolas as separate confirmatory tests, with dataset-specific effects; different prompts and response budgets needn’t estimate identical associations. Repeated participants matter in UWA too if they answer multiple groups. In a categorical choice model, a participant intercept common to all words cancels; participant variation must affect preferences, such as valence or rarity slopes. D1 explicitly ignores Nicolas’s dependence (`scripts/d1/README.md`, “Conditions”).

**4c — Agree with broadening, but the union isn’t automatically the opportunity set.** Freeze independently coded person-descriptive eligibility rules, including senses, participles and ambiguous adjectives. Predefine Anderson-only, expanded-vocabulary and common-norm-coverage sensitivity analyses. Missing prevalence norms must not silently determine eligibility: that can select on familiarity and rarity. The brief requests candidate attributes that could have been produced; the actual denominator remains unspecified (`notes/project-brief.md`, “Strand B”; `analysis_plan.md`, “Data and exclusions”).

**4d — Agree, with safeguards.** Freeze rules after Study 1; blind ambiguous-response adjudication to rarity and hypotheses. Preserve negation, intensifiers and compounds rather than automatically mapping “not friendly” to *unfriendly*. Report exclusions by dataset and group, and by valence where measurable. Prefer an explicit “outside eligible vocabulary” outcome or state clearly that inference conditions on eligible adjective production. The brief already requires reporting losses (`notes/project-brief.md`, “Strand B”, step 1).

**4e — Disagree with the proposed classification.** An interval such as [−.08, −.02] simultaneously supports the directional hypothesis and excludes effects of −.10 or stronger. Separate direction from practical magnitude.

For D1’s positive-loading \(z\), higher values mean **greater prevalence**, despite the “rarity” label; negative coefficients predict H2 (`scripts/d1/README.md`, “Generating model”; “Fitting model, v2”). With \(\theta\) on that orientation, report \(P(\theta<0)\), \(P(\theta<-.10)\), and the interval. If categories are required: meaningful support when the interval lies below −.10; evidence against an effect that large when it lies above −.10; otherwise inconclusive. These assess a strengthened H2, not every positive rarity association. Justify .10 substantively and translate it into predicted response probabilities.

Other important gaps:

- **Likelihood and group content.** Independent negative-binomial cells permit totals incompatible with fixed response budgets and ignore competition among words. Consider categorical/multinomial production, with an outside-vocabulary category; repeated lists may require sequential or clustered choices. Group intercepts alone impose the same expected word ranking everywhere. Add partially pooled group-specific rarity/valence slopes and group–item content variation, then check concentration, zeros and between-group overlap (`scripts/d1/README.md`, “Generating model”; “Fitting model, v1”).

- **Measurement identification.** Two indicators with differently directed biases don’t establish actual prevalence without anchors or restrictions on shared bias. Informative priors supply assumptions, not independent identification. Keep actual-prevalence conclusions explicitly sensitivity-based. Also, Warriner’s rating **SD** isn’t the **SE of its mean**; correct that distinction (`analysis_plan.md`, “Which rarity”; “Strand B as one generative model”).

- **Simulation evidence is preliminary.** Scenario-1 total-effect intervals are [−.332, −.077] at small/r50 and [−.236, −.071] at large/r50: encouraging for direction, insufficient for the proposed .10 threshold (`results/d1/pilot2_fits.csv`, lines 17, 49). The v2.2 checks reuse those pilot seeds and aren’t independent replications (`v22_check_fits.csv`, lines 9, 17). A final-settings mechanism fit still has maximum R-hat 1.081 (`final_settings_check_fits.csv`, line 2).

- **Design checks and priors.** D1 assumes 400 items, 40% coverage and independent Nicolas responses; reassess recovery using the frozen denominator and actual clustering. Include null and threshold-sized marginal effects, flexible valence adjustment, indicator bias and prior sensitivity beyond frequency-only fits. Twenty repetitions give coarse coverage estimates. Distinguish structural non-identification from sampling failure (`scripts/d1/README.md`, “Conditions”; “Reported per coefficient and cell”; “Fitting model, v1”).
