**The diagnosis is right.** [analysis_plan.md:87](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/development/marked-pole-stereotypes/analysis_plan.md:87), “Commonness judges—Framings”, requires a validated human bridge for absolute description ratings. L1’s exception covers ordinal pair comparisons only. L2’s “What counts against the marking claim” therefore commits to an instrument whose warrant the plan currently withholds.

**The bridge is useful, but 60–80 traits isn’t an adequacy criterion.** One judge can anchor model ratings to that judge’s description judgments; linking to the panel additionally assumes transport of judge-specific calibration across framing and morphology. Many traits don’t replace independent judges. Select enough negated items across valence, reading, act type and commonness; “mostly unnegated plus some negated” could leave precisely the diagnostic cells thin. Determine size through calibration and downstream decision precision, as “Commonness judges—Human judges” already requires ([line 91](/Users/brettreynolds/projects/LLM-CLI-projects/papers/development/marked-pole-stereotypes/analysis_plan.md:91)). Validate on held-out descriptions and propagate bridge uncertainty.

**The difference-in-differences estimates a differential framing gap, not identified morphological bias.** Its identifying assumption is that, absent lexical morphology’s influence, word-to-description discrepancies would be comparable across negated and unnegated concepts. Reading, omission, description length and semantic equivalence could violate that assumption. Different raters also introduce judge-by-framing differences. Define δ on the latent commonness scale, allow heterogeneity, and vary departures from the identifying assumption. Partial pooling cannot establish it.

**The decision rule is defensible as a conservative robustness requirement, but demanding agreement from a known biased indicator can make the stronger instrument hostage to the weaker one.** Specify whether “holds” means the same threshold classification throughout δ’s range, on the same supported vocabulary and predictive contrast. Disagreement warrants diagnosis and inconclusiveness, not automatic rejection.

**Protect workload and blindness.** Keep absolute description ratings before any word exposure, preferably in separate sessions; intermixing comparisons risks consistency pressure and carryover (“Protecting the blind coder”; “L1—Descriptions and judges”). Pilot burden.

Without the bridge, report the committed falsifier unavailable. A word-based test with prespecified δ bounds is a legitimate explicitly amended, conditional commitment, not fulfillment of the existing one.
