41:**What associations can and can't show (2026-10-09, after Kilgarriff 2005 and the Codex review above).** Language isn't random, so with enough data any association among rarity, marking and production will differ from zero, and a demonstrably non-random relation "does not support the inference that it is not arbitrary" (Kilgarriff 2005, p. 263). The inference for each effect is therefore its posterior on an interpretable scale, with the curve of posterior probabilities of exceeding a range of magnitudes in the predicted direction (BDA3 §4.5: "the question 'Does θ equal 0?' can generally be rephrased more usefully as 'What is the posterior distribution for θ?'"). The three-way labels (support, evidence against an effect that large, inconclusive) summarize it against reporting benchmarks, one per estimand, which Brett sets by judgement from concrete probability examples before FIXED; they're evaluative, not implied by the hypothesis, and kept separate from the plausible range used in design analysis (Gelman & Carlin 2014 separate "a realistic hypothesized effect size" from "a minimal effect size deemed to be substantively important"; revised 2026-10-09 after a Codex review, `reviews/codex-thresholds-2026-10-09/`). An estimand without a benchmark that has a principled interpretation is reported by its curves alone. For an equal-tailed 90% interval, lying wholly beyond a benchmark means a posterior probability above .95 of exceeding it. A large association still says nothing by itself about mechanism, since confounding and instrument bias can produce one. Measured controls and the D1 and D2 confound scenarios address only the alternatives they specify. L1, L2 and H2 share the word list and the commonness instrument, so agreement among them isn't independent corroboration.

113:**L1: which pole of an opposition carries the negator (pair level; lexical; settled 2026-10-09 after a Gelman pass and a Codex review, `reviews/codex-lexical-2026-10-09/`; human judging was withdrawn the same day, so L1 runs on model judges).** The hypothesis concerns "the marked pole of evaluative oppositions" (brief, lines 14–15), so its marking claim is first a pair-level one: in a root/negation opposition (*selfish/unselfish*, *kind/unkind*), the negated member names the rarer pole. 116:- *Why word-based commonness can't carry L1.* If judges rate negated words as rarer by δ, the predictor shifts by +δ exactly when the negated member is positive and by −δ when it's negative, so the bias produces L1's predicted association under a null. Valence adjustment doesn't remove it, and a sensitivity grid makes conclusions conditional without identifying them. The bias can run either way: a marked form may seem rarer, or a judge may read *un-X* as 'not X' and rate it as the complement of a rare *X*.
117:- *Primary estimand: morphology-free and ordinal.* For each pair, which concept is judged more common in the population the commonness prompts fix, when both poles are given as behavioural descriptions that avoid the words and their roots (responses: either pole, about equal, can't say). It describes the frozen model protocol: it can confirm or contradict a registered prediction about θ_m, but bears on human perceived commonness only under a stated transport assumption. The rarity account predicts a positive contrast, and pairs whose negated member is positive are the ones that separate it from a valence account. It's estimated for the process that generates such pairs (primary, with the pair-generating population stated) and for the frame's own pairs (descriptive), as in D2. Because reading covaries with pair type, θ_m is also reported within reading strata (contrary pairs, contradictory pairs), with counts first and only where both pair types occur in a stratum, and the pooled θ_m is split into within-reading contrasts and reading composition. For exact complements the comparison reduces to whether the root is the majority state. Reading may itself result from lexicalization, so conditioning on it defines the construct; L1's claim stays descriptive. Ordinal comparisons can't recover a slope on logit commonness, and none is claimed. This amends "Framings": for this estimand, description-based judgments are primary data.
