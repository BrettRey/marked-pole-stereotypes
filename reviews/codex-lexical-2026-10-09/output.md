1. **Keep the split, but don’t claim both levels test Haspelmath–Karjus.** The brief commits to an opposition-level claim plus a broader common-cause explanation ([brief:14–15](notes/project-brief.md:14)); its operational H1 is word-level production ([brief:67–71](notes/project-brief.md:67)). The plan conflates pair-level L1 with word-level marking ([plan:34](analysis_plan.md:34), [101](analysis_plan.md:101)). Crucially, the supplied form-frequency principle concerns **usage**, not trait prevalence. Neither lexical test directly tests it. Both require an additional prevalence-to-usage hypothesis; stakes could disrupt that bridge. Section 8’s weak diachronic effect supplies neither an expected English prevalence effect size nor justification for “visible only off the diagonal.”

2. **The bias argument is correct under its stated coding.** Let \(Y=1\) mean positive negated member and \(D\) mean positive-pole rarity minus negative-pole rarity. A negation-induced rarity bias gives \(D_{\mathrm{observed}}=D_{\mathrm{true}}+\delta(2Y-1)\), creating association under a null. Linear or flexible valence adjustment doesn’t remove this direct morphological contamination. Without restrictions on that bias, adjective-only L1 is unidentified. A sensitivity grid makes conclusions conditional; it doesn’t identify them. The word–description gap also includes semantic and framing differences, so it diagnoses instrument dependence rather than estimating \(\delta\) automatically.

   Description comparisons can support a **different L1 estimand**: whether the negated concept is judged less prevalent. They need no absolute-scale bridge, but ordinal comparisons cannot recover the proposed logit-prevalence-difference slope. This requires an explicit amendment to the auxiliary-only rule ([plan:84](analysis_plan.md:84)). Least defensible human work: one blinded judge compares **every pair**, with randomized order and “equal/uncertain” responses; a second blinded judge independently checks a stratified subset, including rare-positive pairs, using independently written, equivalence-checked descriptions. That establishes limited reproducibility, not accuracy about actual prevalence. Fix subset size from precision on disagreement and framing effects; Brett alone cannot provide blind validation. One wording per concept leaves description bias inseparable from concept differences.

3. **L2 rewording is fair only with an explicit reduction in scope.** The existing plan says stakes without prevalence defeats the “single-rarity claim” ([plan:103](analysis_plan.md:103)). A stakes association alone cannot establish that: a rarity effect may coexist, be poorly measured, or lack overlap. Preserve falsifiability by specifying which well-identified prevalence effects would count against the broader common-cause account. Assigning every inconvenient result solely to the word-level account would protect the original explanation without justification. Arousal and extremity also need construct validation as stakes indicators.

4. **Description coding doesn’t resolve act type’s causal position.** It reduces lexical cueing, but omission can remain constitutive of the derived concept. Morphology-free wording isn’t causally prior meaning. Use explicit alternative causal structures and adjusted/unadjusted contrasts on the same population, as H1 already requires ([plan:56–60](analysis_plan.md:56)). Don’t describe coefficient movement as bias removed.

5. **D2 needs selection and model failure.** Include eligibility-dependent selection, root availability, shared roots, near-equal poles, uncertain valence orientation, separation, sparse affix classes, correlated description errors, and floor compression. Simulate mechanisms outside the fitted model, not just parameter recovery. Report false meaningful-support decisions under systematic bias; Type M is undefined at zero. Evaluate standardized contrasts as well as slopes, with fixed scaling across specifications ([Multiverse](analysis_plan.md:63)).

6. **Simplify where the frame cannot support complexity.** Domain-varying slopes plus process effects may be largely prior-driven. A derived-only stratum cannot contribute to the primary pair estimand. Freeze sense-level eligibility and exclusions, retain uninterpretable pairs transparently, and distinguish interval-based evidence against a meaningful effect from evidence of absence.
