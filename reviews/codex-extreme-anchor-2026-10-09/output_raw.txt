**The fix is useful, but supports a qualified extension rather than panel-equivalent anchoring.** Taking Anderson’s exclusion as supplied, my recommendations are design judgments, not established sample-size requirements.

1. **Model each human separately.** Avoid a pooled “expert judge” arm: expertise doesn’t establish population representativeness. Estimate coder-specific thresholds and discrepancies on the overlap, with simple, regularized calibration. Linking identifies their relationship to Ziano *within its support*; it cannot identify an extreme-region discrepancy shared by both coders. Partial pooling mustn’t manufacture confidence about that discrepancy. This preserves the transport concern in [the review, J3–J4](reviews/codex-judges-2026-10-09/output.md:7).

2. **Aim for 60–80 linking traits and 100–150 extension traits per coder.** These are pragmatic starting budgets; fix the final allocation using simulations of calibration precision and downstream H2 bias, as [the plan requires](analysis_plan.md:83). Select linking traits across panel commonness, valence, morphology and semantic domains, including boundary items. Draw extension traits randomly from the frozen eligible vocabulary, oversampling extreme negatives while retaining near-boundary negatives, positive extremes and other unsupported combinations. Balance morphology and provisional act type without forcing a sparse full cross. Record inclusion probabilities; don’t select using stereotype outcomes or model commonness. Extreme negatives alone cannot establish the within-valence gradient H2 needs.

3. **Use the blind coder as primary; Brett as a prespecified sensitivity analysis.** The existing promise that both act-type coders are hypothesis-blind is impossible and needs correction ([plan:58](analysis_plan.md:58)). If feasible, recruit two or three additional blind volunteers, each covering overlap and extension items; adding independent people addresses a limitation that adding words cannot. Randomly intermix linking and extension traits, conceal stratum labels and existing ratings, randomize order independently, and collect commonness before act-type coding, preferably in separate sessions. Preserve independent commonness ratings; don’t adjudicate them into agreement.

4. **Two judges justify conditional H2 use, not an unconditional transport claim.** One blind judge plus Brett supplies limited evidence about human disagreement beyond Anderson. Say: “Expanded-vocabulary H2 estimates are conditional on transport of coder-to-panel calibration and stated bounds on extreme-region shared bias.” Require downstream sensitivity checks before granting that warrant; unsupported cells remain descriptive. The current support rule should distinguish this qualified warrant from validated panel support ([plan:81](analysis_plan.md:81)).

5. **Prespecify failures and distinguish populations.** Check floor compression: if extreme traits all receive minimum ratings, more ratings may not recover their ordering. Retain the perceived-commonness interpretation and wording ambiguity ([plan:79](analysis_plan.md:79)). Compare measurement arms on identical vocabularies; report expanded-vocabulary H2 separately, as required by [the multiverse](analysis_plan.md:69). “Use them all” should never elevate a weak scale bridge into equal evidential standing.
