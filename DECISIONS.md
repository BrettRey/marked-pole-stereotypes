2026-10-07 — D1 pilot 1 stopped after 6 of 26 fits (post-pilot, logged): frequency-only fits fail to converge (R-hat up to 1.76, up to 1,001 divergences), as weak identification predicts, but the scenario-1 fit with a good prevalence indicator (r = .8) also failed (R-hat 1.30, 89 divergences), mostly in βv and βρ. Diagnosis: the non-centered latent prevalence (κ v + √(1−κ²) z) couples κ to every latent value, a parameterization known to sample badly when the latent is strongly informed. Added parameterization options (same model and priors) and a check, `scripts/d1/param_check.py`, committed before it runs; the grid will use the winner. Pilot 1's partial output is kept as `results/d1/pilot1_fits_stopped.csv`.

2026-10-07 — D1 fitting model v2 (post-pilot; spec amended in `scripts/d1/README.md` before v2 ran). The parameterization check showed centring removes divergences, but mixing stayed poor; `diag_corr.py` traced it to an identification ridge, not geometry: with a free valence term in every equation, κ (the share of prevalence that tracks valence) trades off against each equation's valence coefficient, and the sign of κ isn't identified (ESS ~7, correlations up to .92). v2 uses within-valence rarity z as the latent and total valence coefficients; tracked coefficients become βz (H2's within-valence estimand), βm, βf, βvt, γz, γvt, γs, with true values converted exactly. Substantive consequence, worth carrying into the plan: whether valence affects production or marking "directly" rather than via rarity is not identified without an assumption about the prevalence indicator's own valence bias; H2, H1 and L1 as within-valence estimands are.

2026-10-07 — Plan reworded (a reviewing agent flagged it; consistent with the D1 v2 finding): the model identifies within-valence rarity effects (H2, L1, L2) and marking beyond rarity (H1), but not whether valence matters once rarity is known, so the plan no longer promises a rarity-versus-valence contrast. Two routes to restore it are listed for Brett (assumed indicator bias with sensitivity analysis; two prevalence measures with known, different valence biases). D1 v2.1: shear reparameterization of the production equation (sample the total z effect, derive βz; prior and model unchanged) because βz and βf trade off through frequency's loading on z (r = −.93).
