2026-10-07 — Terminology: four senses of "negative" are kept separate in the plan and report: valence (bad; Warriner), formal negation (a negator in the word, coded by affix type: contrary *un-*/*in-*/*dis-*, *non-*, privative *-less*), order markedness (Ingram et al. 2016 probabilities), and scalar polarity (less-of pole; not a markedness measure for H1). UWA's presence/absence coding (πneg = 1 − πpos) is a strand A modelling choice, not a property of words; affixal negation is not treated as feature absence, since *un-* on evaluative adjectives usually gives a contrary. "Negative pole" and bare "marked" are avoided. Owner: Brett (approved in session). Reason: the assistant's reading of UWA had collapsed these senses (equating *unfriendly* with the absence of *friendly*, and posing the negativity-diversity tension as "negative = marked"); Brett asked whether two senses were being conflated. 2026-10-07 — Strand A consensus split run (post-run notes): the spec's chance Jaccard ("about .026") was an approximation; the exact value is .028 for 5-of-100 sets, and when every top 5 is negative the right baseline is 5-of-50 sets, .058. 2026-10-07 — D1 pilot 1 stopped after 6 of 26 fits (post-pilot, logged): frequency-only fits fail to converge (R-hat up to 1.76, up to 1,001 divergences), as weak identification predicts, but the scenario-1 fit with a good prevalence indicator (r = .8) also failed (R-hat 1.30, 89 divergences), mostly in βv and βρ. Diagnosis: the non-centered latent prevalence (κ v + √(1−κ²) z) couples κ to every latent value, a parameterization known to sample badly when the latent is strongly informed. Added parameterization options (same model and priors) and a check, `scripts/d1/param_check.py`, committed before it runs; the grid will use the winner. Pilot 1's partial output is kept as `results/d1/pilot1_fits_stopped.csv`.

2026-10-07 — D1 fitting model v2 (post-pilot; spec amended in `scripts/d1/README.md` before v2 ran). The parameterization check showed centring removes divergences, but mixing stayed poor; `diag_corr.py` traced it to an identification ridge, not geometry: with a free valence term in every equation, κ (the share of prevalence that tracks valence) trades off against each equation's valence coefficient, and the sign of κ isn't identified (ESS ~7, correlations up to .92). v2 uses within-valence rarity z as the latent and total valence coefficients; tracked coefficients become βz (H2's within-valence estimand), βm, βf, βvt, γz, γvt, γs, with true values converted exactly. Substantive consequence, worth carrying into the plan: whether valence affects production or marking "directly" rather than via rarity is not identified without an assumption about the prevalence indicator's own valence bias; H2, H1 and L1 as within-valence estimands are.

2026-10-07 — Plan reworded (a reviewing agent flagged it; consistent with the D1 v2 finding): the model identifies within-valence rarity effects (H2, L1, L2) and marking beyond rarity (H1), but not whether valence matters once rarity is known, so the plan no longer promises a rarity-versus-valence contrast. Two routes to restore it are listed for Brett (assumed indicator bias with sensitivity analysis; two prevalence measures with known, different valence biases). D1 v2.1: shear reparameterization of the production equation (sample the total z effect, derive βz; prior and model unchanged) because βz and βf trade off through frequency's loading on z (r = −.93). Committed before running.

2026-10-07 — D1 v2.2 (post-pilot, committed before running): the prevalence indicator's measurement-error SD is fixed at its known value, as for a norm averaged over raters. v2.1's shear and low-rank adaptation didn't fix mixing (third similar sampler-level attempt, so that line was stopped); correlations pointed to the indicator's loading and error SD, i.e. a one-strong-indicator factor model whose true-variance/error split isn't identified. Requirement carried to the prevalence-norm search: item-level standard errors (or rater counts and SDs), not just means.
