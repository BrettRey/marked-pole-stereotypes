2026-10-07 — Terminology: four senses of "negative" are kept separate in the plan and report: valence (bad; Warriner), formal negation (a negator in the word, coded by affix type: contrary *un-*/*in-*/*dis-*, *non-*, privative *-less*), order markedness (Ingram et al. 2016 probabilities), and scalar polarity (less-of pole; not a markedness measure for H1). UWA's presence/absence coding (πneg = 1 − πpos) is a strand A modelling choice, not a property of words; affixal negation is not treated as feature absence, since *un-* on evaluative adjectives usually gives a contrary. "Negative pole" and bare "marked" are avoided. Owner: Brett (approved in session). Reason: the assistant's reading of UWA had collapsed these senses (equating *unfriendly* with the absence of *friendly*, and posing the negativity-diversity tension as "negative = marked"); Brett asked whether two senses were being conflated. Detail in `analysis_plan.md`, Variables.

2026-10-07 — Plan revision (Brett: "proceed", after asking what Gelman would do): strand B becomes one generative model with trait prevalence as a latent variable and word frequency, any prevalence indicators and stakes indicators as noisy measures; H1, H2, L1 and L2 become coefficients of it. A design simulation (D1: parameter recovery, plus a mechanism check that simulates from strand A's process) runs before any outcome data, and its results decide whether a prevalence indicator is essential per coefficient. This replaces the earlier "reported as not run" rule and the assistant's proposed one-sided word-frequency fallback, which was significance-filter reasoning (Gelman & Carlin 2014). Word frequency enters as a modelled path, not a regressed-out covariate (Gelman & Hill 2007, §9.7). Simulated data is used only to evaluate the design, never as evidence.
