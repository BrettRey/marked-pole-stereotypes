# Lexical frame and commonness: decisions for the next stage

Adopted with Brett’s refinements, 2026-10-11. The source import and 40-form pilot are
complete. This document specifies the next bounded stage: settle the units
and judgment targets, apply them to the existing pilot, and test mapping and adjudication for the six proposed pairs. It doesn't declare the full vocabulary frozen or start commonness
collection. All new judgments remain model judgments.

**Adopted decisions.** Adopt a resource-attested adjective frame, preserve
distinct interpretations, and give enduring characteristics and temporary
states different commonness questions. Use a US reference population for
the description instrument, explicitly as a design choice. Keep the
source-matched human calibration task intact. The population choice and the
separate temporal targets are the main decisions for this review.

**What enters the frame.** Retain the Anderson/Warriner union and the broad
psychological scope already accepted for curation: personality,
interpersonal behaviour, psychological states, dispositions and capacities.
Extreme and temporary meanings remain eligible. Exclude physical-only,
object-only, membership-only, institutional-status-only and record-only
meanings. Apply the rule to the supported interpretation of an adjective
word, rather than assigning one category or meaning to every source spelling.

Use WordNet 3.1 first. Apply the same pinned English Wiktionary supplement
rule whenever WordNet supplies no supported interpretation in the study’s
scope, or leaves a relevant category, interpretation or pair-alignment
decision unresolved. This includes every form without a WordNet adjective.
Log each trigger before retrieval; retain unresolved cases after the fixed
second source rather than searching indefinitely. Successful retrieval
of entries containing only other lexical categories means **outside this
resource-attested adjective frame**. Failed retrieval, a missing entry or
ambiguous category evidence means **unresolved coverage**. Neither label
asserts that an adjective word cannot exist elsewhere. Don't gate eligibility
on a spelling's most frequent category.

The existing preparation manifest records 3,786 forms with WordNet adjective
evidence among 14,082 imported forms. That leaves 10,296 requiring the
consistent supplement route before this rule could define the full frame.
That is the count under the narrower category-gap trigger. Additional
in-scope and unresolved-evidence triggers enlarge the full-pass workload
by an as-yet uncounted amount. These are dictionary-coverage counts, not counts of eligible psychological
adjectives. Supplementary retrieval and evidence review therefore belong in
the full-pass budget; applying the pilot's model-call price to every raw form
would misdescribe the work.

**What counts as a unit.** Keep this chain explicit:

> source row → normalized form → category-specific lexical item →
> supported interpretation → judgment target → presentation

*Adjective* names a category. Attributive modifier and predicative complement
name functions. An adjective and a noun with the same spelling remain
different words. Preserve dictionary-supported distinctions between
same-spelling adjective words too. Where the evidence leaves the boundary
between homonymy and polysemy unresolved, retain alternative groupings;
don't manufacture extra units by counting synsets.

Each interpretation records its source sense IDs, eligibility, temporal
target and any grouping decision. Several synsets can support one target;
one synset can require a split, as with personal trustworthiness versus
assigned accountability under *responsible*. Within a word-level analysis
specification, each lexical item has one unit of weight. Alternative
interpretations define alternative specifications. Repeated wordings and
model calls don't add lexical units.

An unglossed word judgment remains attached to its lexical cue. It cannot
inherit a selected interpretation merely through a spelling join. Likewise,
a production response whose meaning cannot be resolved remains one observed
response with ambiguous mapping; it isn't copied into several sense rows.
Shared spellings, words and bases remain recorded for dependence modelling.
Pair-analysis weighting is a separate outstanding estimand decision: equal
weight per pair and equal influence per lexical item need not agree.
Dependence modelling does not decide between them. Fix this before commonness
collection; preserve both member identities and the pair graph now.

**Population and time: the adopted choice.** For description-based
judgments, use **people living in the United States, without an added
adult-only restriction**, in **present-day life, with 2026 fixed as the
reference year**. The year dates the intended context; it isn't a request
to reconstruct a measured 2026 prevalence statistic. Every later run would
retain that same reference year unless the protocol were explicitly revised.

Why the US? The existing calibration instrument refers to the average
American. Keeping a US reference in the description task reduces a stated
population difference when comparing instruments. This is a practical scope
choice, not a claim that the hypothesis concerns only the US or that the
human anchor validates description judgments. The source's average-American
wording also leaves demographic composition underspecified; the two tasks
still aren't identical populations or constructs.

The credible alternative is a geography-neutral question about people in
general. That would define a broader, less determinate model protocol, and
it would require a separate interpretation of word/description differences.
The explicit US scope is adopted for the first description protocol.
Neither choice measures an actual population or recruits anyone from it.

**Keep the temporal questions separate.** All descriptions in a comparison
must share their population, temporal target and relevant respect.

| Target | What the model compares | Proposed question stem |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enduring characteristic or psychological capacity | People for whom the description applies as a general characteristic or capacity in the reference context | Which description applies to more people as a general characteristic or capacity? |
| Temporary psychological state | Person-moments, with each person given equal weight and then a waking moment selected in the reference context | At a randomly selected waking moment, which description is more likely to apply? |
| Source-matched display frequency | The original word-rating task | Preserve the original Ziano instructions, item wording and seven response categories |

For the state task, give every person in the reference population equal
weight, then select a moment uniformly from that person’s waking time in
the reference context, including unusual situations as well as routine
ones. Ask whether the state applies at that moment. It doesn't ask whether the person has
ever experienced the state, or how often they display it. No twelve-month
recall window or thirty-day observation window is imposed.

Report L1's characteristic/capacity and state results separately. The broad
lexical frame retains both; a single pooled contrast would mix different
targets. An interpretation that supports both receives separately identified
targets and enters separate specifications. Don't reinterpret a state as a
disposition just to complete a pair. Cross-target alignments remain recorded
but cannot enter a same-target pair comparison. Within each temporal and
reading specification, an L1 contrast requiring both morphological
orientation types is unavailable if one type is absent. Report the available
descriptive results; do not add words or substitute zero.

For the source-matched arm, load QID63 and QID65 from the pinned final Ziano
survey verbatim, retaining its own temporal indeterminacy. Its definition
concerns how frequently the average American would display a characteristic.
Changing it into a share-of-people question would change the calibration
task. Absolute description ratings retain their separate, unanchored
model-judged status; their final scale and judge panel require specification
before commonness collection.

**Pair meanings before pair readings.** Both members must be independently
eligible adjective words in the candidate sources, with compatible
interpretations and the same temporal target. The immediate base is the
pairing partner. Nominal-base privatives such as *careless/care* remain
outside the primary adjective-pair frame, even though *careless* can enter
word-level analyses. Missing counterparts aren't added to improve counts.

First check domain and sense alignment. Record each pole’s application
domain and whether the domains coincide. Inapplicability because a relevant
situation or capacity is absent differs from falsity within that domain;
inapplicability does not establish the lexical opposite. Only then ask
whether both concepts, or neither, can apply to the same person at the same
time and in the same respect **within the recorded domain**. If exhaustiveness
holds only during waiting, or in a relevant subgroup, retain that restriction.
For later commonness comparisons the stated population and temporal sampling
scheme remain the denominator. A conditional comparison within an applicable
subgroup or situation is a separately identified target, not a silent change
of denominator. A dictionary's *not* or *lack* wording
doesn't settle this. Record the evidence and the two answers separately;
derive a contrary/complementary label only when they support it. Preserve
overlap and undetermined cases explicitly.

The pilot's *patient/impatient* case illustrates why these steps must be
separate: eagerness doesn't automatically oppose even-tempered endurance.
The saved family proposals contain 16 distinct sense-ID alignments across
the six provisional pairs, including proposals that need rejection or
splitting. They aren't 16 independent pairs or 16 endorsed alignments.

For disputed readings, retain source-compatible alternatives as separate
description specifications. A constructed exact complement that the lexical
evidence doesn't support may serve as an instrument diagnostic, but it
cannot silently become the word's meaning in the primary lexical analysis.
If no defensible description target can be fixed, retain the pair in the
unresolved table. This tightens the older blanket instruction to give every
disputed case both readings; it doesn't select whichever reading yields the
predicted commonness result.

**Description construction and ratings.** Once a target and its admissible
reading alternatives are recorded, obtain two independently written
descriptions per pole from the two specified model families. Avoid the
lexical labels and their bases. Check both fidelity to the supplied evidence
and equivalence between wordings. An exact-complement branch must negate
the same predicate, including its context and threshold, rather than
substituting an independently written opposite.

Commonness judges receive only the population/time instruction, descriptions
and target-specific response options fixed as follows.

| Task | Directional responses | Equality response | Uncertainty response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Characteristic or capacity | A applies to more people / B applies to more people | They apply to about the same number of people | Can’t say |
| Temporary state | A is more likely to apply at the selected moment / B is more likely to apply at the selected moment | They’re about equally likely to apply | Can’t say |

About equal is a substantive judgment and can’t say records inability to
judge; neither label defines a numerical equivalence margin. Word labels, morphology, source ratings and the research
prediction stay out of those packets. Counterbalance side and order in
fresh contexts, retaining the settled filler and shared-error requirements.
This specifies the intended task, not a claim that lexical cueing or shared
model bias has disappeared.

Keep Anderson person-likableness and Warriner word valence separate. Later
orientation of a selected interpretation requires a source-task compatibility
record, including unresolved and potentially mismatched cases. Uncertain
orientation affects the analysis specification; it doesn't erase an
otherwise eligible word or pair. Report estimated sizes, uncertainty and
changes across specifications, following Brett's settled direction without
introducing new substantive cutoffs.

**The bounded implementation after review.** Encode these fields and apply
the mapping to the existing 40 forms. Retain all original pilot answers and
adjudications. Prepare one compact packet for each of the six provisional
pairs, containing its dictionary evidence and all saved candidate alignments,
without earlier verdicts. These alignments are proposals, not an exhaustive
menu: the reviewer may reject all or add alignments supported by the supplied
evidence. Ask each of the existing two model families to
check alignment, temporal compatibility and reading, in separate contexts.

That is **12 calls maximum**, one attempt per pair/family, with a five-minute
limit per call and at most two calls running concurrently. The packets no
longer combine 40-word discovery with reading adjudication. Use the same
verified Codex and Claude subscription routes, model versions and high-effort
settings as the pilot. No API credit purchase or HF spending is involved.
The time ceiling is 60 summed call-minutes, excluding preparation and review.

Completion and valid evidence references establish whether the smaller
instrument is workable. Agreement isn't its success criterion; unresolved
readings are valid results. A timeout or malformed response remains a
recorded failure, without another call to chase an answer. Audit every
returned alignment against its supplied evidence and the written target.

The resulting report will show the 40-form mapping, six pair dispositions,
remaining ambiguities, actual service time/usage and review work. Then prepare
a full-frame workload and resource budget, including supplementary lookup,
source review and revisions, broader supplement triggers, rejected analyses,
plausible unmatched forms and candidate generation independent of the detector.
The six-pair stage does not estimate missed-pair recall. Full-vocabulary coding and commonness collection
remain later stages. The running simulation recovery is independent of this
work and its scientific source files remain unchanged.

      ### 1. Remove the possible restriction introduced by *ordinary life*

      The state instruction currently asks for a waking moment in the person’s *ordinary life*. That phrase could lead a judge to exclude crises, celebrations, illness, or other unusual situations. This matters because the frame deliberately retains extreme states. lexical-protocol-decisions-2026…

      I’d specify selection from the person’s **waking time in the reference context, including unusual situations as well as routine ones**. Also make *uniformly* explicit for the selection of a moment, just as equal weighting is explicit for people.

      The resulting instruction should establish:

      - Each person in the reference population receives equal weight.
      - A moment is selected uniformly from that person’s waking time in the reference context.
      - Unusual moments remain eligible.
      - The question concerns whether the state applies at that moment.

      That resolves the sampling interpretation without adding a recall period or requiring the model to reconstruct a measured prevalence statistic.

      There’s a substantive choice here: a protocol restricted to routine situations could be legitimate, but it would define a narrower target. *Ordinary life* shouldn’t introduce that restriction incidentally.
